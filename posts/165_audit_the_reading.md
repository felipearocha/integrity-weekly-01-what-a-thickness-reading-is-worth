# Before You Replace the Spool, Audit the Reading

Friday 2026-11-20

[165_audit_the_reading.pdf](../media/165_audit_the_reading.pdf)

A CML drops from 6.4 mm to 5.8 mm in four years, and a spool replacement lands in the turnaround budget. Before it does, audit both readings.

The case is illustrative but familiar. An 8 inch line, required thickness 5.0 mm. The long term rate had been 0.05 mm/yr. The new short term rate is 0.15, remaining life falls from 28 years to about 5, and the planner does the sensible thing with the number in front of them.

The usual wrong move is to argue about which rate to use. The better first question is whether the two readings were taken the same way.

Here they were not. The 2021 reading was single echo, through about 0.25 mm of paint. Sound is slower in paint than in steel, so the gauge, which assumes steel, turns that film into roughly 0.6 mm of extra wall. The 2025 reading was echo to echo, which excludes the coating. Corrected, the 2021 wall was 5.8 mm. The loss in four years is nothing the data can show.

API 570 5.7.1 asks for exactly this care: "Inspectors should be aware of possible sources of measurement inaccuracies and make every effort to eliminate their occurrence." Its list starts with calibration, external coatings or scale, surface roughness, probe placement, laminations and temperature.

The slides walk through the audit: what the gauge really measures, six checks to run on both readings, and what to record so the next engineer can run them.

On Monday, pull the five CMLs with the largest gap between short and long term rate, and check the mode, coating and temperature of each reading.

Which of those does your database record?

Reference: API 570, 4th Edition (2016), 5.7.1 UT and RT; API 510 5.7.2.4 carries a similar list for vessels. The case is illustrative, with sound velocity in the paint assumed at 40% of steel.
